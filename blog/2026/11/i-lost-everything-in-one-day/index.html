I Lost Everything in One Day and it wasn't the first time.

8 November 2026 · 2 min read · Đọc bằng tiếng Việt

Then I Started Building TeachersDeck.

I want to tell you how TeachersDeck began. It didn't start in a startup office. It started in a hotel room in Saigon, on the worst day I've had in Vietnam.

First, a little about me

I'm Jerry, an American from Honolulu, Hawaii. I've been teaching in Saigon for four years. Most expats I know who stay here long enough end up opening a bar or a restaurant. I never wanted that. I wanted to do something for kids.

The widget page that stayed with me

It began at a workshop run by the education company I teach for. Someone showed a webpage full of classroom widgets: a clock, a timer, a stoplight. Nothing fancy. But I kept thinking about it on the ride home. Every teacher I know reaches for tools like these all day long. What if someone built them the way teachers actually work?

I had no developer and no budget, so I started building with Claude.ai on the free plan. When I hit the free limit, I opened another Google account and kept going. I did that across more than ten accounts before I could finally afford a monthly Pro subscription. It was clumsy, but it was moving.

The day it all went wrong

Then, in a single day:

  • The monsoon rains flooded my house. I lost everything except a few clothes and my motorbike.
  • I was kicked out of my apartment.
  • My school schedule was delayed because the city was restructuring its English curriculum, so I had no classes for the new school year.

I spent three days in a hotel room. I was out of money, out of work, and honestly tired of being a poor teacher. Then a friend found me a place to stay, in a preschool.

Somewhere in those days I made a decision. I would stop waiting for the next contract and put my energy into something that could outlast me. I started calling it my life's legacy.

One app became a whole family

The first video I made promoting TeachersDeck

I thought I was building one tool. Then I kept asking, "What would a student need? A parent? The person running the school?" So TeachersDeck grew into StudentDeck, ParentDeck and AdminDeck. Then I thought about universities, and LectureDeck, UniDeck, UniStudentDeck and UniParentDeck followed.

I don't want to replace the big learning management systems that schools already use. I want to sit next to them. That's why there are now versions of TeachersDeck for ClassIn, Canvas and Moodle.

That all happened in about two months of working nearly every day. One app turned into more than ten.

Where I am now

I'm still teaching, and I'm still building. I'm not doing this for the glamour. I'm doing it because I believe a good set of tools can make a teacher's day easier and a school run a little smoother, and kids benefit from that.

If you work in a school or language center, I'd love to hear what you wish existed. Leave a comment, or send me a message.

Thanks for reading.

Jerry, Saigon
